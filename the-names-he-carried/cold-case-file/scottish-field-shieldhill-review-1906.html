"SCOTTISH FIELD" for November.

That beautifully illustrated magazine "The Scottish Field" easily maintains premier position among the monthly publications appealing to public patronage, and this month's issue presents to the reader one of the choicest selections of contributions that one could wish, the wonder being how the promoters are so fortunate in being able to secure such a steady supply of high-class matter.

One of the prominent features is an illustrated description of Shieldhill, the residence of Mr Nathaniel Dunlop, the genial chairman of the Clyde Navigation Trust. Shieldhill is a place of many memories, and is one of the most picturesque and interesting in the Upper Ward of Clydesdale. Since he acquired it a few years ago, Mr Nathaniel Dunlop has effected many improvements, building a tower over the modern wing, and adding to the same side a new dining-room in the sixteenth century style. But none of the old outside features has been interfered with. On the west side of the house is preserved the old doorway of the sixteenth century, with tirling-pin and massive key, just as it was when Queen Mary's supporter locked it against his enemies. Above it, built unto the wall at a more recent time, is a stone, probably an altar-piece from the ancient chapel inside, sacred initials I.H.S. and two ecclesiastical symbols, with on one side the shield of the Chancellors, and on the other that of the Symingtons, one of whom was lady of Shieldhill when the old peel tower was repaired.

Robert Gordon's College, Aberdeen, is the school selected for treatment this month, from which we learn that the founder was born in 1665 and amassed a considerable fortune abroad, returning to his native city in 1720, where he lived in retirement till his death in 1731.

The angling section of the magazine contains several notable contributions.

There is also a varied selection of general articles, such as "Guns and Shooting," "Notes on Rugby Football," "A Little-Known Island," "Blue Bonnet's St. Leger," and many others.

"The Scottish Field" may be had from all bookstalls, newsagents, &c., or direct from the head office, 11 Bothwell Street, Glasgow, price 6d, or by post 7½d. Altogether it is a splendid publication and thoroughly worth the money charged.

What it says

The November 1906 Scottish Field carried “an illustrated description of Shieldhill,” the home of Mr Nathaniel Dunlop, chairman of the Clyde Navigation Trust.

It credits Nathaniel with building a tower over the modern wing and a new dining-room on the same side in sixteenth-century style. It describes the old west doorway with its tirling-pin and massive key, and a stone above it, “probably an altar-piece,” with the initials I.H.S., two church symbols and the shields of the Chancellors and the Symingtons.

The story of Queen Mary’s supporter locking the door is the paper’s own; it is not proven history.
