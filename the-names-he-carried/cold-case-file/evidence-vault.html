The scale of the investigation

An Extraordinary Family Investigation

What began as an effort to understand one man's life has grown into an extensive historical investigation spanning generations, countries, institutions, and more than a century of surviving records.

Researchers have assembled civil registrations, census returns, passenger manifests, medical and hospital records, newspaper accounts, estate histories, photographs, maps, family testimony, and original archival documents. Together, these sources reveal the people and communities surrounding Laurence Brown Douglas, born Lawrence Brown in Edinburgh on June 14, 1907.

The investigation reaches far beyond conventional genealogy. It examines the Scottish estate where his mother lived, the influential family who owned it, the circumstances of his birth, the household that raised him, and the journey that eventually brought him to America.

The Evidence Vault opens this extraordinary collection to the public. Visitors can inspect the documents, trace the sources, examine the discoveries, and follow the questions that remain unanswered. This is not simply a family tree. It is a reconstruction of a life through the surviving historical record.

And we're still discovering more.

The standard of proof

Evidence first. Conclusions second.

Birth certificates establish recorded births. Census pages identify enumerated households. Newspaper reports document what was published about public events. None of those sources, alone, establishes a hidden motive or biological paternity. Where the record stops, the investigation says so.
