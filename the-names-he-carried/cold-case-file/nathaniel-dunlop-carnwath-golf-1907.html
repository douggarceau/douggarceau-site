OPENING OF THE GOLF COURSE.
FASHIONABLE CEREMONY.

An event of more than usual interest to Carnwath and district took place on Gallow Hill on Saturday afternoon in the most auspicious of summer weather. From about three o'clock could be seen the constant arrival of motor cars, carriages, cyclists, and foot passengers to the new pavilion on the golf course, erected near the first green. The climatic conditions being ideal, the large concourse of people, numbering several hundreds, presented an animated and brilliant spectacle.

Punctually at 3.30 Sir Simon and Lady Macdonald Lockhart arrived, having come down from London specially for the occasion.

James Williamson, Esq., J.P., D.L., of Westsidewood, on behalf of the members of the Golf Club, presented Lady Lockhart with a silver mounted golf club suitably inscribed. Mr Williamson paid high tribute to Sir Simon and Lady Lockhart for the interest they manifested towards anything which was for the good of the community. Lady Lockhart he eulogised in no half-hearted manner—her beauty and grace, her friendly manner and spontaneous affection making her the admiration of all. Mr Williamson also called to remembrance that exactly sixty one years ago the mother of the present Sir Simon Lockhart cut the first sod near Carnwath for the putting through the Caledonian Railway. The Railway had been a complete success, and had now become a necessity all over the world, and likewise a golf course was looked upon as a necessity by every town and village, and he was sure that the Carnwath Golf Club should be no less a success opened under such distinguished patronage. Lady Lockhart thereupon thanked the members through Mr Williamson for the beautiful gift, and wished the golf club a successful career, also for the flattering terms in which he had spoken.

Lady Lockhart then drove off the first ball with a sweeping stroke, and declared the course open.

Sir Nathaniel Dunlop, of Shieldhill, who was amongst those to receive the King's birthday honours, also spoke befitting the occasion. Although not having previously met Sir Simon and Lady Lockhart, he yet had more than passing interest in them, having known Sir Simon's mother in childhood, both being brought up at Campbeltown.

Rev. D. Hay Sawers thanked Lady Lockhart for her kind services in coming to Carnwath and opening the Golf Course.

Sir Simon, in replying for Lady Lockhart, said it gave them no small amount of pleasure to come and do anything which was for the good of the place, and hoped that it would be one long continued success.

Three hearty cheers were then given. Tea and refreshments were served in the pavilion and in the open air. The pavilion is a handsome building, standing on an eminence near the public road, and is a notable landmark for many miles round. It contains waiting rooms, lockers, lavatory accommodation, etc., and was designed and built by Mr Wm. Smith, Kaimino [?], Carnwath. An exhibition game was played of 18 holes—twice round the course—by two well known professionals Willie Auchterlonie, winner of Open Championship in 1893, and A. Gourlay. During the first round Gourlay played a steadier stroke, and maintained his lead, Auchterlonie having the worst of bad luck on three occasions. On the second round Auchterlonie settled down to steady play, and maintained his form until the finish, the score being equal.

The course—a full description of which has already appeared in the papers—consists of nine holes with plenty of natural hills and hazards, which alike please and torment the man who golfs. Situated as it is five minutes walk from the village and Railway Station, and within easy walking distance of Carstairs Junction, it is easily accessible from all parts, and should be the means of bringing many more visitors to the historic village. With commendable promptitude the Committee have already made arrangements with the Caledonian Railway Coy. for a late train to call at 8.15 for the convenience of golfers returning to the Capital.

What the record says

Sir Nathaniel Dunlop of Shieldhill, newly named in the King’s birthday honours, spoke at the opening of the Carnwath golf course on Gallow Hill.

He said he had not met Sir Simon and Lady Lockhart before, but had known Sir Simon’s mother in childhood, “both being brought up at Campbeltown.”

The paper calls it Saturday afternoon. Carnwath Golf Club’s history gives Saturday, June 29, 1907, when Lady Hilda Lockhart opened the course. The newspaper came out on July 6.

Limits

It places Nathaniel at this one event. It says nothing about the rest of his day, about Agnes, or about Laurence’s parentage. The knighthood announcement published the same day is a separate record.
