1934 border card: Elizabeth Anderson comes to America

U.S. Immigration and Naturalization Service, Form I-448, port of Port Huron, Michigan, October 2, 1934. Card Manifests of Individuals Entering through the Port of Detroit, M1478. Image via Ancestry.

The entries are marked in red and highlighted.

What this document is

A U.S. immigration card written for each traveller who came into the United States from Canada. The inspector recorded name, age, birthplace, last home, the relative being joined and a description; the back holds the inspector’s remarks.

What it says

  • Anderson, Elizabeth; age 60; born Edinburgh, Scotland; housekeeper; last permanent residence Dunfermline, Scotland.
  • Nearest relative left behind: a cousin, Mrs. Jenny Doig (Ancestry's index reads Daiz), Rose Cottage.
  • Going to her daughter, Mrs. Janet Crombie, 1518 Kostner Avenue, Chicago, for permanent residence; she paid her own passage and carried $60.
  • Five feet five, fair complexion, fair hair, blue eyes.
  • Landed at Quebec from the Letitia on September 30, 1934; admitted at Port Huron, October 2, 1934.
  • On the back: money being forwarded from Dunfermline to her daughter in the U.S.; examined and found admissible at Quebec.

Why it matters

Lizzie Douglas, the woman who raised Laurence, followed her children to America. In 1934, at sixty, she came to Chicago to live with Janet, a few doors from Laurence on Kostner Avenue.
