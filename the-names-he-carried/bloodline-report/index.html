Bloodline Report: Laurence’s Mother’s Line Traced Back to the 1760s

Research notes, not part of the book · records only

Laurence’s family tree on his mother’s side used to stop at his great-grandparents, a Peeblesshire ploughman and his wife. Record searches now carry it back six generations. The line runs through the Beild, an old inn in the Tweedsmuir hills, to farming families in Dumfriesshire in the 1760s.

Correction

An earlier version of this report traced the line further back, to the Goldies of Craigmuie, a provost of Dumfries, the Covenanter Gordons of Earlstoun and others, as far as 1479. That was wrong. It rested on a family tree on Ancestry that treated two different men named James Goldie as one person. The Kirkmichael baptism register shows that Laurence’s ancestor James Goldie was still having children baptised in 1772. James Goldie of Craigmuie died in 1763. Those older lines have been removed.

The line, from Laurence back

  1. Mother: Agnes McGarth Brown, born December 18, 1885, at Edston near Peebles.
  2. Grandfather: George Brown (1862–1886), ploughman. His death register names his parents.
  3. Great-grandparents: William Brown, ploughman (died 1895 at Stobo), and Agnes McGarth (1840–1924), born at the Beild, Tweedsmuir.
  4. Great-great-grandparents: Tweedie McGarth (1811–1876) and Jean Goldie (1808–1891), who kept the Beild.
  5. Third great-grandparents: Alexander Goldie (baptised 1762, died 1822) and Agnes Mundell (1768–1849), farmers in Kirkmichael, Tinwald and Wamphray, Dumfriesshire. On Tweedie’s side, Michael McGarth and Margaret Larkin.
  6. Fourth great-grandparents: James Goldie and Ann Wilson of Kirkmichael, and John Mundell and Mary Anderson of Cleughbrae, Mouswald.

The Beild

Tweedie McGarth and Jean Goldie kept the Beild, an old coaching inn on the Edinburgh–Dumfries road below Oliver Dod in the Tweedsmuir hills. Tweedie was innkeeper and shopkeeper, and from 1847 the Post Office messenger between Broughton and Tweedsmuir. His wife’s obituary remembers his home-made mail cart, which he called the Chariot, and his donkey, Omar Pasha. Jean ran the Beild for more than fifty years and died there on Christmas Eve 1891. They are buried together at Tweedsmuir Kirk. Their daughter Agnes married William Brown, a ploughman, at the Beild in 1858, and their son George was Laurence’s grandfather.

The Goldies of Kirkmichael

Jean Goldie was born at Wamphray in 1808, a daughter of Alexander Goldie and Agnes Mundell. Alexander was baptised at Kirkmichael on June 6, 1762, the son of James Goldie and Anne Wilson. James and Ann had at least five children baptised at Kirkmichael between 1760 and 1772: Agnes, Alexander, James and two sons named Richard. Alexander farmed at Upper Mains in Kirkmichael, at Dalruscan in Tinwald, and at Langside and Newtown in Wamphray. He raised ten children and died at Wamphray in 1822. Agnes Mundell, the eldest of eight children of John Mundell and Mary Anderson of Cleughbrae, Mouswald, died at Newtown, Wamphray, in 1849.

What is known, and what is not

  • From records: each step from Laurence’s mother back to James Goldie and Ann Wilson, taken from civil and parish registers (as indexed on Ancestry), the Tweedsmuir gravestone and Jean Goldie’s obituary.
  • Not yet known: James Goldie’s own parents, birth, work and death; Ann Wilson’s family; the parents of Michael McGarth and Margaret Larkin.
