Bloodline Report: Laurence’s Mother’s Line Traced Back to the 1600s

Research notes, not part of the book · every claim marked by how sure we are

In one day of record searches, Laurence Brown Douglas’s family tree grew from his great-grandparents to his seventh great-grandparents, nine generations in all. The trail started with a middle name, McGarth. It ran through a coaching inn in the Tweedsmuir hills, down into the farms of Dumfriesshire, and ended in the town houses of Dumfries in the early 1700s: a provost, a judge of the wills court, and the daughter of a Galloway laird.

The headlines

  • Nine generations. Laurence’s mother’s line is now traced to people born in the 1650s and 1660s.
  • A provost. James Corrie of Speddoch, his sixth great-grandfather, governed Dumfries in the 1710s, 1720s and 1730s.
  • A judge. John Goldie of Craigmuie, his fifth great-grandfather, was Commissary of Dumfriesshire for more than forty years.
  • A laird’s daughter. Mary Gordon, his sixth great-grandmother, was a daughter of Sir Alexander Gordon of Earlstoun.
  • A Bank of England question. Jean Paterson, his seventh great-grandmother, was said in family tradition to be related to William Paterson, who founded the Bank of England. She was not his sister. A cousin is possible, but not proven.
  • An inn and a donkey. His great-great-grandparents kept the Beild at Tweedsmuir. The postman of the family drove a mail cart called the Chariot, pulled by a donkey named Omar Pasha.

How the trail was followed

The key was a name. Laurence’s mother was registered as Agnes McGarth Brown, and her father, George Brown, gave her his own mother’s name. George’s death register named his parents, William Brown and Agnes McGarth. Agnes McGarth’s baptism at Tweedsmuir named her parents, Tweedie McGarth and Jean Goldie. Jean Goldie’s baptism at Wamphray named hers, Alexander Goldie and Agnes Mundell. From there a family tree on Ancestry, built by another descendant (PamelaWills909’s Cairns Family Tree), carried the Goldies back through Kirkmichael to the Goldies of Craigmuie and the Corries of Dumfries. Each step was then checked against gravestones, published family histories and archive catalogues.

Generation by generation

1. Laurence’s mother: Agnes McGarth Brown, born 1885

Born at Edston near Peebles on December 18, 1885, the second daughter of George Brown, a ploughman, and Christina Henderson. Her father died when she was three and a half months old, and she grew up in the cottages at Quothquan. In 1907, as a domestic servant, she registered her son, Lawrence Brown, in Edinburgh. Her middle name, McGarth, is the thread that leads to everything below.

2. Grandfather: George Brown, 1862–1886

A ploughman, one of at least ten children of William Brown and Agnes McGarth. He married Christina Henderson at Quothquan in 1883 and died at Edston at 23. His death register is the document that names his parents, and it opened the McGarth line.

3. Great-grandparents: William Brown and Agnes McGarth, 1840–1924

William was a Peeblesshire ploughman who died at Stobo in 1895. Agnes was born at the Beild, Tweedsmuir, on January 20, 1840, and married William there in June 1858. She died at Innerleithen in 1924, aged 84. New today: her birth, baptism and marriage were confirmed from the Tweedsmuir registers. An old index had spelled her name McGrath, but the register says McGarth.

4. Great-great-grandparents: Tweedie McGarth (1811–1876) and Jean Goldie (1808–1891)

The couple kept the Beild, a former coaching inn on the Edinburgh–Dumfries road below Oliver Dod. Tweedie was innkeeper, shopkeeper and, from 1847, the Post Office messenger between Broughton and Tweedsmuir. His wife’s obituary remembers his home-made mail cart, the “Chariot,” and his donkey, Omar Pasha. Jean ran the Beild for more than fifty years and died there on Christmas Eve 1891. New today: photographs of the Beild and of the couple’s gravestone at Tweedsmuir Kirk; Jean’s full 1892 obituary; the couple’s twelve children; and Jean’s birth at Wamphray, Dumfriesshire. That was the step that carried the line south into Dumfriesshire.

5. Third great-grandparents: Alexander Goldie (1762–1822) and Agnes Mundell (1768–1849); Michael McGarth and Margaret Larkin

Alexander was baptised at Kirkmichael, Dumfriesshire, in 1762. He farmed at Upper Mains in Kirkmichael, at Dalruscan in Tinwald, and at Langside and Newtown in Wamphray, raising ten children, and died at Wamphray in 1822. Agnes Mundell grew up at Cleughbrae, Mouswald, the eldest of eight. On Tweedie’s side, his parents were Michael McGarth and Margaret Larkin, named in his 1811 baptism. Nothing more is known of them yet.

6. Fourth great-grandparents: James Goldie (1737–1763) and Ann Wilson; John Mundell and Mary Anderson

James was the eldest son of the Goldies of Craigmuie. In 1761 he was made factor to the Duke of Queensberry, and he died two years later, aged 26. The family gravestone confirms his dates. Caution: this is the weakest link in the chain. An Ancestry tree makes him Alexander’s father, with Ann Wilson as Alexander’s mother, but the 1899 Corrie family history doesn’t name a wife for James. The 1762 Kirkmichael baptism entry needs to be read to confirm it.

7. Fifth great-grandparents: John Goldie of Craigmuie (1708–1776) and Jean Corrie (1712–1783)

John was Commissary of Dumfriesshire, the judge of wills and estates, from 1734 for more than forty years. Jean was the daughter of the provost of Dumfries. Their gravestone in Dumfries names them both, and their eldest son James.

8. Sixth great-grandparents: Edward Goldie of Craigmuie and Mary Gordon; James Corrie of Speddoch and Agnes McKitrick

Edward married Mary Gordon in 1701 and died in 1711. Mary’s gravestone at Balmaghie calls her the daughter of Sir Alexander Gordon of Earlstoun. Her second husband was the Covenanting minister John Macmillan. James Corrie was a Dumfries merchant, Provost of Dumfries from 1718 into the 1730s, and laird of Speddoch. His first wife, Agnes McKitrick, a merchant’s daughter, died in 1721 at 31.

9. Seventh great-grandparents: Sir Alexander Gordon of Earlstoun and Janet Hamilton; William Corrie and Jean Paterson; James McKitrick

Sir Alexander and Janet Hamilton married in 1676. William Corrie of Mirehouse was a bailie of Dumfries. He married Jean Paterson in 1677, had eleven children, and died in 1709. James McKitrick was a Dumfries merchant in 1710. These are the furthest-back ancestors found so far on any line of Laurence’s family. They were born around the 1650s and 1660s, about 250 years before Laurence.

The gravestones

Three stones carry much of the weight. At Tweedsmuir Kirk, Tweedie McGarth and Jean Goldie are buried together, with the children they lost young. In Dumfries, the Goldie stone reads: “JOHN GOLDIE, Esq. of Craigmuie and Commissary of Dfrs. … JEAN CORRIE, his wife, daughter of JAMES CORRIE, Esq. of Speddoch … JAMES, their eldest son, born 1737, died 1763.” At Balmaghie in Galloway, Mary Gordon’s stone begins: “Here ly the Remains of Mistress May Gordon, daughter of Sir Alexander Gordon of Earlstoun.”

The Bank of England question

A 19th-century book, The Scottish Nation, calls Jean Paterson the sister of William Paterson (1658–1719), the Dumfriesshire man who founded the Bank of England. The records don’t support that. Paterson’s 1718 will names his only surviving sister as Elizabeth, and an 1865 study of his family names his sisters as Janet and Elizabeth. The Corrie family’s own historian, writing in 1899, called Jean “a near relative” of Paterson but could not say how. One real link survives: William Paterson owed Jean’s husband, William Corrie, £45. The verdict for now is a possible cousin, not proven.

What is solid, and what is not

  • Solid: Agnes McGarth Brown to George Brown to Agnes McGarth to Tweedie McGarth and Jean Goldie (civil and parish registers, gravestone).
  • Solid: Jean Goldie’s parents, Alexander Goldie and Agnes Mundell (Wamphray baptism register).
  • Needs checking: whether Alexander Goldie was the son of James Goldie of Craigmuie. Everything from James back depends on this one link.
  • Solid, on that condition: the Goldies of Craigmuie, the Corries of Speddoch and the Gordon of Earlstoun connection (gravestones and Records of the Corrie Family, 1899).
  • Open: Jean Paterson’s relationship to William Paterson; the parents of William Corrie, Michael McGarth and Margaret Larkin.

Corrections made today

  • The Ancestry tree puts Kirkmichael in Ayrshire. This Kirkmichael is in Dumfriesshire.
  • The tree shows Alexander Goldie and Agnes Mundell marrying at Middlebie in 1871, decades after both had died. That entry is an error.
  • Find a Grave names Jean Goldie’s father as William. Her baptism names him Alexander.
  • The tree gives children to James Goldie born after his death in 1763. They belong to a different James Goldie.

What comes next

  • Read the 1762 Kirkmichael baptism entry for Alexander Goldie to confirm his parents.
  • Ask Dumfries and Galloway Archives about the Goldie of Craigmuie papers, 1655–1857.
