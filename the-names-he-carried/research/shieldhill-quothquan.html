Why these places matter

Agnes Brown gave Gean Cottage, Shieldhill, Biggar as her home when she registered Laurence’s birth. The birth record connects the family to the place. The sources below describe its institutions and suggest where further evidence might survive.

The material reviewed here identifies no baptism for Laurence, no attendance record for Agnes, and no first-hand statement identifying the father. A source about a hall or minister supplies context; a named family entry would answer a different question.

Building identification

Two halls, with separate histories

Shieldhill Hall / Blue Hall

HES: Shieldhill Hall, LB12995 records a 1903 worship hall funded by Nathaniel Dunlop and Miss E. Dunlop: corrugated iron, one storey, projecting wings, timber gable details and a roof ventilator.

Official location: NS 99544 39695. Category C; listed 21 May 1991. The 1991 description expressly says the interior was not inspected.

Quothquan Village Hall

The Parish history describes a former manse, later joiner’s shop; electricity in 1951; recreation rooms transferred in 1956; trustees: four Bowling Club and four WRI officers.

The Community photograph collection distinguishes the former-manse hall from Dunlop’s “Blue Hall.” Use the pictured building and the source location when matching an old reference to “the hall.”

A minister named in a contemporary report

The Hamilton Advertiser, 21 July 1906, page 5, reports the Girls’ Friendly Society visit on 11 July. It names Rev. C. Ross Lowdon conducting a short service in a hall after the visitors arrived at Quothquan. The party then went to Shieldhill for tea.

The printed surname is Lowdon; the official minister list uses Lowden. The report does not name the hall, identify Agnes among the visitors or establish the arrangements for regular Sunday services.

Addresses and local memory

The village around the family

Gean Tree and Lime Tree

Parish history says Geantree incorporated Lime Tree; no merger date is given.

That statement is a lead about the buildings, not proof that their households were combined in 1901 or 1907. Compare the Henderson household and Mary Archibald’s address with the surviving census entries and dated plans.

Andrew Henderson

The Community photograph collection remembers Andrew Henderson of Lime Tree as a poet and beekeeper; poems were reportedly untraced. Matching name and address make this relevant, but the identification with Agnes’s uncle still needs corroboration.

Huntfield Place and the smithy

The Community photograph collection associates Dunlop’s 1898 improvements with six houses becoming four and a raised, slated smithy house. Its historic photograph identifies Huntfield Place numbers 2–4.

Use the historic view alongside the estate profile; unidentified figures remain unidentified.

Two local-history book leads

The council page credits Tom Brown. The Parish bicentenary account credits David Brown with compiling a comprehensive church history. Titles, editions and holdings still need confirmation; the names should not be merged.

The bicentenary account also describes historical objects lent for a parish display. This suggests a route to locating surviving local collections, not a promise that a particular family photograph exists.

The NRS parish collection history dates the Libberton church building to 1812 and restoration to 1902. The Parish art gallery provides five views of church art, including stained glass and an angel relief.

How to search the church material

Scotland’s People: kirk session records explains that baptisms, accounts, transfers and correspondence may occur inside minute books. Confirm the volume list, then browse the relevant dates through Scotland’s People: Virtual Volumes or the holding repository.

Record the volume, page or folio and entry date. Search Lawrence/Laurence; Brown/Douglas; Agnes McGarth/McGrath; Robert and Elizabeth/Lizzie Douglas; Henderson; Parker; Gean Tree/Geantree and Lime Tree. Expand the years only after documenting the first search.

Laurence’s move to the Douglas household

NRS: adoption records dates Scottish legal adoption to 1930 and states that the Adopted Children’s Register has no entries for births before October 1909. Laurence was born in June 1907.

Investigate the earlier care arrangement through any surviving family, church, hospital, charity or poor-relief papers. No document explaining who arranged the move was identified in this review.

Parish-council and poor-relief records

NRS: poor-relief records directs researchers to local archives for parish-council records after 1894. South Lanarkshire: county archives places the main county collection at Glasgow City Archives; its own listed poor-relief holdings cover 1930–48.

First establish whether Libberton applications, registers, minutes, correspondence or boarding-out records survive for 1906–08. A parish-level reference for that period has not yet been confirmed.
