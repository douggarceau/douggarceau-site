The Dunlops

Dr. James Dunlop, M.D. (about 1834–1901)

Brother of Sir Nathaniel Dunlop of Shieldhill · father of Dr. Binnie Dunlop

James Dunlop was a Glasgow doctor born in Kintyre, near Campbeltown. He was Sir Nathaniel Dunlop’s brother and Binnie Dunlop’s father. He died at his home, 16 Carlton Place, Glasgow, on Wednesday, March 13, 1901, aged 67.

The brothers

His obituary in the Campbeltown Courier of March 16, 1901 says he went to Glasgow “with his brothers Archibald, Nathaniel, and Matthew.” The three older brothers went into business, and James entered Glasgow University. This is a newspaper of the time naming Nathaniel as his brother, in so many words.

His life and work

His hometown paper, the Campbeltown Courier, called him Professor James Dunlop. He went to Campbeltown Grammar School, took his M.D. at Glasgow in 1857, and worked at the Glasgow Royal Infirmary under Joseph Lister. He held the professorship of surgery at Anderson’s College for about twenty years and was a surgeon of the Royal Infirmary for forty. He was Medico-Legal Adviser to the Crown in Glasgow’s great criminal trials, surgeon of the Lock Hospital, a governor of the Samaritan and Victoria hospitals, and consulting surgeon to the Caledonian Railway and the Clyde Pilot Board. He pushed for vaccination and isolation in a smallpox epidemic, and was a volunteer officer for forty years with the 3rd Lanark Rifle Volunteers, rising to Brigade-Surgeon-Lieutenant-Colonel.

At 67 he volunteered for the South African War. In January 1900 he sailed as acting surgeon on the troopship Bavaria, paying his own way, and worked six to seven months in the surgical wards of the Wynberg Base Hospital near Cape Town.

The Glasgow Medical Journal obituary (April 1901) calls him one of the busiest men in the city, with many public posts and a large private practice, until failing health made him cut back. He was admitted a Fellow of the Faculty of Physicians and Surgeons on June 4, 1860, and spent his whole working life in Glasgow. In his spare time he studied the natural history of deep-sea fish on his yearly visits to Kintyre, writing on them in the Glasgow Herald, and he lectured on ancient Egypt. His published papers included notes on post-mortem examinations in criminal cases for the Crown in Glasgow (1893) and an address on the Victoria Infirmary (1896).

Family

He married Jane Menzies Binnie. Both obituaries say he left a widow, six sons and two daughters. His eldest son was in business in London; his second son, Dr. Binnie Dunlop, was serving as a civil surgeon with the army in South Africa; and his third son was a barrister in London (Campbeltown Courier, March 16, 1901).

Death and funeral

A newspaper report of March 18, 1901 describes the funeral on the afternoon of Saturday, March 16, 1901, from his house in Carlton Place to Sighthill Cemetery. Mourners named in the North British Daily Mail include ex-Bailie Dunlop, Nathaniel Dunlop, Matthew Dunlop and Sir James Bell. Binnie, then in South Africa, is not named.
