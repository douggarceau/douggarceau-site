The Dunlops

Dr. Binnie Dunlop (1874–1946)

Nephew of Sir Nathaniel Dunlop of Shieldhill · son of Dr. James Dunlop · one of the two men examined in the Cold Case File

Binnie Dunlop was born on August 3, 1874, to Dr. James Dunlop and Jane Menzies Binnie, and qualified in medicine at the University of Glasgow (M.B., 1898). He was Sir Nathaniel Dunlop’s nephew. Because the family remembered Laurence’s father as a doctor, Binnie is one of the two men the Cold Case File examines. No record yet places him at Shieldhill or near Agnes.

Eugenics and the Malthusian League

After qualifying, Binnie Dunlop held house-physician and house-surgeon posts at the Victoria Infirmary, Glasgow. He served as a civil surgeon in the South African War, and as a temporary captain in the Royal Army Medical Corps in the later part of the First World War (British Medical Journal obituary, July 27, 1946, p. 142). C. V. Drysdale’s obituary in the Eugenics Review says he never engaged in practice, which may mean he never kept a private practice. Most of his working life went to the population question. He joined the Malthusian League in 1910 and served as its honorary secretary and treasurer, and he edited its paper, The Malthusian, from 1918 to 1921. He was probably the anonymous author of the League’s 1913 pamphlet on birth control. Reference works describe him as an advocate of eugenics, and his obituary appeared in the Eugenics Review in October 1946.

His own published letters show what he believed. In them he wrote:

“Although the poor and the unfit continued ‘propagating their kind unchecked’… Hence the reversed selection which you rightly deplore.” (January 1913)

“…they bring into the world several times that number, half of whom grow up more or less defective, and half of whom die prematurely.” (January 1913)

“It seems to me most urgent—on humanitarian, eugenic, and social-unrest grounds… that we immediately begin to encourage the married poor to avoid bringing more children into the world…” (September 1913)

“…doctors and nurses must realise that it is dysgenic, as well as cruel, to leave parents ignorant of family limitation…” (February 1914)

“…the extending of this knowledge to the wage-earners… is—on humanitarian, eugenic, and anti-socialistic grounds—by far the most urgently needed reform of the day.” (April 1914)

Each quotation is from the full letter, transcribed word for word, in the Cold Case File below.
