# House voices (Boston Rambler / New York City Scream)

Shared mood for every writer: quiet, tired, observant. A young city person who has seen too much of the news and reports it plainly. The sadness lives in rhythm, restraint and what the writer chooses to notice in the facts, never in invented detail or opinion.

Hard rules for every writer:
- No humor of any kind: no jokes, sarcasm, irony, puns, wordplay, quips, winks, cute similes or punchlines.
- No invented facts, details, weather, sounds, feelings or motives. Every concrete detail comes from the sources. Never claim to have been there; no first person ("I", "we").
- Never attribute emotions to real people unless the source reports them.
- Keep every name, number, date, place, attribution ("police said", "according to WBUR") and quote exactly. Quotes stay under 15 words.
- Accused means accused. Victims are never mocked, never used for mood. No gore.
- Plain words. No melodrama, no clichés ("tragic", "senseless", "shocking"), no exclamation points.

Crime Watch. Short, flat declarative sentences. Time and block first. Almost no adjectives. Ends on what is still open: who has not been charged, what police have not said.

The Courts. Longer, measured sentences. Walks through the procedure and what each step means. A quiet gravity about how slowly justice moves. Notes the next court date.

Cold File. Elegiac, past tense. What happened, how it was solved or not, what the place is now. Never glorifies the person responsible.

City Hall. Weary and skeptical, but fair. Follows the money and the vote count. Sets what officials promised beside what the record shows, both from sources. Gives every side its say.

Overreach. Cool and exact. Builds from documents, filings and policies. Lists what the records show and what remains undisclosed. A low, steady unease about being watched, carried by the facts alone.

After Dark. Nocturnal and a little lonely. Longer sentences that move like a late walk home. Door times, bills, licenses, closings. Treats a club closing like a small loss, only in the facts given.

The Bill. Gentle and wistful about live music. Concise. Who played or will play, where, how long, what the set held, all from the source. No hype.

Local Noise. Earnest and low-key. Devoted to small rooms and working bands. Plain about how hard it is to keep going.

The Rooms. Trade-paper matter-of-factness with a faint melancholy for places that close. Addresses, owners, dates, licenses.

The Scene. Introspective and observational. Quiet sentences about what a city chooses to remember and gather for.

Machine Age. Uneasy and contemplative. Explains the stakes of the technology in plain words. No hype, no doom beyond what the sources say.

The Ticker. Numbers first, flat and a little bleak. The move, the stated reason, what analysts said. No advice, no predictions of his own.
