Meet the Drum Set

A basic drum set has four or five drums (bass drum, snare, one or two rack toms and a floor tom) and two or three cymbals. Every drummer sets up a little differently, but the parts are the same. This book uses a four-piece kit.

PARTWHAT IT DOES
Bass drumThe big drum on the floor, played with your right foot on a pedal. It is the low heartbeat of the band.
Snare drumThe drum between your knees. Metal wires (the snares) under the bottom head give it its crack. Most backbeats are played here.
Rack tomMounted above the bass drum. Higher-pitched, used mostly in fills.
Floor tomStands on its own legs to your right. Deep and booming.
Hi-hatTwo cymbals on a stand with a pedal. Played with sticks, with your left foot, or both. Closed it is tight and crisp; open it sizzles.
Ride cymbalA large cymbal on your right used to “ride” a steady pattern, especially in jazz.
Crash cymbalUsed for big accents, usually at the start of a new section.
EXAMPLE 2.1A tour of the kit
TEMPO

Each drum and cymbal in turn: bass drum, snare, rack tom, floor tom, hi-hat, ride, crash, hi-hat foot.

Left-handed?TIP

Many left-handed drummers set up a mirror-image kit: bass drum on the left foot, hi-hat on the right. Everything in this book works the same way; just swap “right” and “left”.
