The Reggae One Drop

In the one drop, the bass drum skips beat 1 entirely and lands with a cross-stick on beat 3. Carlton Barrett of Bob Marley and the Wailers made it famous, and the empty downbeat is what gives reggae its laid-back float.

Hear it on: Bob Marley and the Wailers, Survival (1979)

How it counts

Hi-hat on every swung eighth note. Cross-stick and bass drum together on 3, nothing on 1. Count 1 & 2 & THREE & 4 &.

Learn it in three steps

  1. Swung hi-hat alone. Long-short, long-short, like a relaxed triplet.
  2. Add the cross-stick on 3: rest the stick across the snare and click the rim.
  3. Add the bass drum on 3 with it. Resist the urge to play on 1.

Watch out for

  • Sneaking a bass drum onto beat 1. The whole style lives in that empty space.
  • Rushing. The one drop should feel like it is sitting back.
